Team Name : DigiIndia
Team Leader Name : Yashika Soni
Problem Statement : To develop and demonstrate an algorithm to predict energetic
particle fluxes of electrons at geostationary orbit. The algorithm should be able to 
predict the harsh radiation fluxes at least 30 to 45 minutes in advance, and also give a 
reasonable forecast for 6 hours and 12 hours ahead.

  KAVACH
• Existing/typical approaches treat this as a single 
black-box ML regression problem: solar wind in → 
one neural network → flux out, with one accuracy 
table at the end.
• KAVACH instead runs two physically independent 
forecasting engines in parallel, a data-driven 
Temporal Fusion Transformer and a first-
principles radial-diffusion physics solver and uses 
their agreement/disagreement as a real, physically 
grounded uncertainty signal, the way operational 
centers like NOAA SWPC reason about ensemble 
forecasts.
• It also explicitly solves the data-scarcity problem 
most teams will ignore: instead of training a weak 
model directly on ISRO's limited 1-2 years of 
GRASP/GSAT data, it pretrains on 11 years of 
GOES and transfers to the Indian-longitude payload 
turning a stated dataset limitation into a 
demonstrated engineering result.
• It includes a regime-aware layer (quiet / storm-
onset / main phase / recovery) so the system adapts 
its behavior to the dominant physical process at 
each moment, rather than forcing one model to fit all 
conditions equally.
How different is it from any of the other 
existing ideas?
• Reads and processes 11 years of 
GOES >2 MeV electron flux and Wind/OMNI 
solar wind data from CDF archives; applies 
despiking, log-transformation, gap handling, 
and physics-informed feature engineering 
(IMF coupling function, dynamic pressure, 
ULF wave power proxy).
• The dual-engine ensemble produces multi-
horizon forecasts simultaneously 30 minutes, 
6 hours, and 12 hours ahead exactly as 
required, with quantile/probabilistic outputs 
instead of single-point estimates.
• A risk-translation layer converts forecasted 
flux + uncertainty into a Green/Yellow/Red 
operational index based on known 
deep-dielectric charging thresholds, giving 
ISRO satellite operators a directly actionable 
output rather than a raw number.
• Validated against real historical storm events 
(onset → peak → recovery) and cross-
checked against ISRO's own GRASP/GSAT 
data for the Indian longitude sector.
How will it be able to solve the problem?
• Hybrid physics + AI, not AI alone 
and defensible scientific grounding, 
not just curve-fitting.
• Calibrated uncertainty as a feature, 
not an afterthought - operators get 
confidence, not just a number.
• Built-in domain transfer to 
ISRO's own payload despite limited 
local data.
• Decision-ready output (risk index) 
directly usable in satellite operations, 
not just a research metric.
• Explainable: attention weights tie 
forecast drivers back to known 
radiation-belt physics at each time 
horizon, directly demonstrating 
scientific understanding alongside 
technical execution.

List of features offered by KAVACH
04
Decision & explainability layer
• Green/Yellow/Red operational risk index based on deep-dielectric charging thresholds
• Attention-based driver explainability, showing which solar wind variable dominates at each forecast horizon
01
Data ingestion & physics-informed feature engineering
• Automated reading and visualization of GOES electron flux and Wind/OMNI solar wind CDF archives (11 years)
• Built-in cleaning: despiking, log-transformation, gap interpolation
• Physics-derived features: IMF coupling function, dynamic pressure, ULF wave power proxy, lagged flux history
05
Cross-cutting capability
• Transfer learning from GOES (11 yrs) to ISRO's GRASP/GSAT data (1–2 yrs) for Indian-longitude validation
• Storm-replay validation suite benchmarking against persistence and calibration plots
02
Dual-engine forecasting core
• ML engine: Temporal Fusion Transformer producing simultaneous 30-min / 6-hr / 12-hr forecasts
• Physics engine: a numerically integrated radial diffusion solver, run independently as a first-principles cross-check
• Quantile/probabilistic outputs : confidence ranges, not single-point guesses
03
Regime-aware ensemble
• Automatic classification of magnetospheric state (quiet / storm-onset / main phase / recovery)
• Ensemble fusion where ML-physics agreement or disagreement becomes a real, physically meaningful 
uncertainty signal

Technologies to be used in KAVACH :
Data ingestion & processing
• Python - primary language for the full pipeline
• cdflib / SpacePy - reading GOES, Wind, and OMNI CDF archive files
• pandas, NumPy - time-series alignment, resampling, cleaning
• SciPy - interpolation, signal filtering (despiking), and the numerical ODE solver for 
the radial diffusion physics engine
Forecasting core
• PyTorch - primary deep learning framework
• PyTorch Forecasting / Darts - Temporal Fusion Transformer implementation and 
multi-horizon quantile forecasting utilities
• scikit-learn - baseline models, scaling, evaluation metrics (RMSE, PE)
• SHAP / built-in attention extraction - explainability layer (driver importance per 
forecast horizon)
Validation & visualization
• Matplotlib / Plotly - flux time series, storm-replay plots, calibration curves
• MLflow (or Weights & Biases) - experiment tracking across model versions and 
hyperparameter runs
Application & serving layer
• FastAPI - Risk API serving forecasts and risk classifications
• PostgreSQL / TimescaleDB - time-series storage for processed flux, solar wind 
features, and forecast history
• React (or Streamlit for a faster build) - operator dashboard for live risk monitoring
• Docker - containerized deployment of the full pipeline for portability to ISRO 
infrastructure
Supporting
• Git/GitHub - version control and team collaboration
• Jupyter - exploratory analysis and model development during R&D phase
